    Guide

    The Herculaneum scrolls are rolls of papyrus that the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 AD carbonized. They are too fragile to unroll, so the Vesuvius Challenge scans them with X-ray CT, and people and programs trace the wound sheet in 3D to unwrap it virtually. A scroll is wound around a center. The center line, or umbilicus, follows that center from the bottom of the scan to the top. Tracing tools start from it, so a good line for every scan saves everyone time.

    This page draws every public center line we know of for the 22 scans on the prize page (the 21 First Letters volumes and PHerc1447, read 23 October 2026): 29 lines from 5 sources.

    How to look around

    1. Array stands every scan's lines side by side at true height. Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, hover for names. One row holds the lines made by people, the other the Center Finder's lines.
    2. Click a scan (in the 3D view or the list) to open its X-ray: a radiograph your browser builds from CT it streams from the open data, with the lines inside.
    3. The square at the bottom right is one horizontal slice through the scan. Drag its slider or scroll over it to move up and down. Rings mark where each line crosses that slice; a good line sits in the middle of the innermost turns.
    4. Lasagna shows villa's sheet model on PHerc0175A: where it thinks papyrus is, and which way each sheet faces.
    5. Data opens every number on this page as tables you can sort and download.

    Colours and grades

    Ice lines were made by people. Amber lines are model output from Chase Hendrick's Center Finder, made for the nine 1.2 m scans nobody had traced; no person has checked their ends on CT. An automatic end check graded each Center Finder line:

    • PASS both ends passed.
    • FLAG it found a problem at an end: a lean, or a line that stops short of the papyrus.
    • VOID the tilt test failed its own control on that scan, so the check tells us nothing either way.

    Each line's card says which of its ends are usable. A faded line is one its author withdrew from recommended use.

    A center line is geometry. Nothing here is a reading of any text.

    Every line is a JSON file in SharpMeow/vesuvius-center-lines: a list control_points of {"x", "y", "z"} in the level-0 voxels of the scan the file names, plus a metadata block in most files. Each scan card has a download link per line, a copy URL for its raw file, the scan's OME-Zarr URL, and copy x,y,z for the crossing on the slice you are looking at.

    VC3D

    VC3D (volume-cartographer in villa) reads an umbilicus JSON that is a bare array of points, or an object with points or control_points; every point needs x, y and z. It also reads an optional metadata block: voxelsize_um, volume, and the grid volume_width, volume_height, volume_slices, all three or none. 22 of the files here carry that stamp, matching their scan's level-0 grid; the others are bare points. Between control points VC3D interpolates linearly; past the last point it holds that point's x and y.

    Source: villa main, volume-cartographer/core/src/Umbilicus.cpp, read 23 Oct 2026.

    villa spiral fitting

    villa's spiral tutorial (last updated 16 Sep 2026) calls the umbilicus required: fit_spiral.py takes a --dataset root and reads umbilicus.json from it, beside lasagna_inputs/*.ome.zarr and the other inputs, and infer_winding_volume.py takes --umbilicus (or finds it from the checkpoint's dataset). Every input has to be in the fit's own voxel frame.

    spiral-fitting's JSON reader sorts control_points by z, multiplies x, y and z by coordinate_scale (or 1/downsample_factor), ignores metadata, and extrapolates linearly past both ends. The files here are level 0, so a fit on pyramid level L needs a scale of 1/2L. Because VC3D holds the ends and the fit extrapolates them, the same file can behave differently above and below its last points: check the line's usable ends first.

    Sources: the tutorial above; villa main, spiral-fitting/umbilicus.py, read 23 Oct 2026.

    villa lasagna

    villa's lasagna predict3d is, in its README's words, "the Lasagna cos/normal wrapper around the shared 3D tiled inference code". The PHerc0175A field in the Lasagna view is its output on the whole scan, in villa's own OME-Zarr layout: SharpMeow/pherc0175a-lasagna.

    How to read them
    
    
      
      
    • Where a scan has two lines, the gap shows how far two independent tracings disagree. It does not say which one is closer to the true center.
      • 
      
    • A line's own spread (stray, wiggle) is not an error. Scrolls are crushed and bent, so the center can wander; a straight line through a bent scroll would be wrong.
      • 
      
    • The end check grade applies only to Center Finder lines. Person-made lines have no grade here; that is not a pass.
      • 
      
    • The radiograph is a view, not a measurement: it uses scan level 5 (32 times coarser than level 0), with a z stride, and a display curve.
      •
