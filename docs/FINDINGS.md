# Findings

Reviewed 23 October 2026. This is the full text behind the README's [Novel findings](../README.md#novel-findings), where each finding has a one-line summary. Every finding keeps its number, so a reference elsewhere to "finding N" still points at the right one. Each states its date, its claim label ([NOVELTY.md](NOVELTY.md)), its limits and its corrections; negative results and corrections stay on the record. Every number with its settings and source is in [`results.json`](results.json).

Public labeled data only, never target scrolls. An ink map is model output, not a reading, and no letters are claimed. Novelty is provisional within our searches (the web and the community repositories we read; GitHub search and Discord were not searchable from here). Reproductions of others' results are in [`results.json`](results.json) and the [community scan log](logs/2026-10-07-community-scan.md).

## Tool results

15. **The scroll center finder's held-out lines sit a median 0.73 mm from the published center lines, and on two sealed lines it was level with villa's `vc_gen_umbilicus`** (13 and 22 October 2026, Model output; rules fixed before each run). Over 3,414 held-out slices of the 13 published lines, each scroll scored by models that never trained on it, the median distance to the published line was 0.73 mm, against 3.98 mm for the papyrus centroid control. The published lines of PHerc0139 and PHerc1451 were never used to train or tune either tool; on their 1.2 m scans, at 525 heights, the median distance was 0.56 mm for the finder, 0.81 mm for `vc_gen_umbilicus` (villa `13a88a6`, seed 1, one repeat) and 2.64 mm for the centroid. Per height the finder was closer on PHerc0139 (median -0.46 mm, 95% interval -1.09 to -0.16) and level on PHerc1451; the rule for "better" needed both scrolls, so the result is level overall, not a win. Limits: each reference is one person's line, and people disagree by more than this error; the 13 Oct settings were chosen while looking at some of the same scrolls; whether either annotator started from `vc_gen_umbilicus` output is unknown. [Tool page](tools/umbilicus.md#checks-and-controls), [guide](../scripts/umbilicus/README.md#held-out-results).
16. **StrokeNet told text from the same text turned 90 degrees far better than any simple score, and passed 7 of the 9 bars of its first preregistered held-out test** (14 October 2026, Model output on six held-out PHerc. Paris 4 tracings, labels made by a rule). On text against its 90 degree turn it scored AUC 0.994 against 0.865 for window texture, ahead on 6 of 6 segments, while its artifact control stayed near chance (0.559). It passed text against blank papyrus at 0.990, where plain ink density did as well (0.996). It missed two bars: blank papyrus against its own turn (0.639, where the bar asks for within 0.10 of 0.5), which suggests part of its sense of orientation reads the direction of the papyrus or the map rather than the writing (Interpretation), and the blank false-positive rate (0.021 against a bar of 0.02). Its simpler two-density score, finding 18, later came out ahead of ink density on PHerc0500P2. [Model card and results](strokenet.md).
17. **Inkfilter's fixed face-mix filter scored above alpha compositing on held-back windows, but only on the segments it was developed on** (17 October 2026, Model output). On 56 confirmation windows from 28 publicly labelled segments across seven scrolls, face mix scored mean AUC 0.736 against 0.704 for the selected alpha-compositing baseline and 0.628 for its reversed-depth control. The confirmation windows are new windows of the same development segments and scrolls; alpha alone wins on three scrolls and the reverse filter on one. This is a model-free filter, not a comparison with trained readers. [Package README](https://github.com/ChaseHendrick/Scrolls/blob/submission/progress-2026-10-inkfilter/submissions/progress-2026-10-inkfilter/README.md).
18. **StrokeNet's two-density score, which needs no network, came out ahead of ink density inside segments on PHerc0500P2 with ink_9um** (22 October 2026, Model output; rule fixed before any test map existed). The score adds a quarter of the ink density around a window (about 6 mm) to the window's own density (about 2.2 mm). On 11 PHerc0500P2 segments, with ink_9um seed 42 as the preregistered reader, its mean within-segment AUC of text against blank was 0.701 against 0.662 for plain density; the mean gain was 0.040 (95 % interval 0.028 to 0.050), ahead on 11 of 11 segments, and both controls passed (reverse depth 0.574, shifted labels 0.507). Seed 43, scored only as a replication, also put the score ahead on 11 of 11 segments, but its reverse-depth control failed, so it is not evidence. Limits: ink_9um reads this scroll weakly (density 0.66), so the gain says nothing about strong readers; one scroll, one reader, one read; three segment pairs share papyrus, so the interval is likely too narrow; the wide window also sees segment edges and holes; the labels come by rule from the team's finer-scan ink map, not from people. [Model card section](strokenet.md#the-two-density-score), [tool page](tools/strokenet.md).
19. **Three hosted text models restored hidden Greek letters in Philodemus, De Musica, less often than a Kneser-Ney letter model** (22 October 2026, Model output; rule frozen before any request). On the second half of De Musica (First1KGreek `tlg1595.tlg221`, Kemke), 190 gaps of 1, 2, 3, 5 or 8 letters inside 150-symbol windows were filled by an order-8 Kneser-Ney letter model (16 million symbols of public Greek, mixed with a component trained on another Philodemus text) and by three hosted models at temperature 0. Gap letter accuracy was 0.331 for the letter model, 0.181 for DeepSeek-V3-0324, 0.181 for Kimi-K2-Instruct-0905 and 0.118 for Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507; the one-sided bounds of the differences were -0.206, -0.207 and -0.275, so the preregistered verdict was FAIL (no model beat the letter model). On single letters the letter model restored 0.895 against 0.447 for the best hosted model, and it was ahead at every gap length; at 8 letters every arm was low (0.066 to 0.132). A memorisation probe found no recall of the text (about 0.08 for every arm). 690 requests cost 0.066 USD, and the public code reproduces the frozen files byte for byte. Limits: one text and one edition, edited text with word spaces rather than papyrus damage, three models with one prompt each, one run; the letter model always returns the right number of letters; the test half had been scored once before in an earlier unpublished letter-model evaluation. Prior work restores Greek with trained networks (Pythia, Ithaca, Apollo Restore) and with n-gram and BERT-like models for the Herculaneum papyri (LREC 2026). Interpretation: we found no published comparison of these hosted chat models with a letter n-gram model on Philodemus. [Preregistration and result](prereg/2026-10-22-greek-restoration.md), [tool page](tools/greekfill.md), [frozen files](evidence/greek-restoration-20261022/README.md).

## Pipeline and reader checks

12. **A transient 404 from S3 becomes a permanent hole of zeros in VC3D's renders and traces** (measured 2026-10-08, reported 2026-10-12 as [villa #2058](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/issues/2058), fix in draft [villa #2060](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/pull/2060)). From one machine, the open-data bucket answered `404 NoSuchBucket` for keys it holds on 11 to 32 % of new connections. VC3D caches any 404 as an empty chunk and serves it as zeros in every later run: a PHerc0139 w035 crop rendered with 27.7 % zero samples, exit code 0 and no warning. With the draft fix on villa `main` at `25f3c93`, the same crop with 6 replayed false 404s matches the clean render, where `main` leaves 12 empty markers and 19.18 % zeros that survive a rerender; the new tests pass with the fix and fail 70 checks on `main`, on Linux and macOS. The false 404s in those renders are replayed by a local relay ([`scripts/false404/fault_relay.py`](../scripts/false404/fault_relay.py)); the fix is not merged, and markers already in users' caches stay wrong. [Log](logs/2026-10-12-upstream-reports.md), [receipts](evidence/2026-10-12-false-404/README.md).
13. **villa's tracer does not hold an explicit seed, so a trace can start on the next winding** (measured 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-12 on public geometry, no ink; reported 2026-10-12 as [villa #2059](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/issues/2059)). On PHerc0139, a `vc_grow_seg_from_seed` seed 1.1 voxels from published winding w031 ends its first 10 generations with none of 324 vertices on w031 and 265 on w033, while `meta.json` still records the seed. Holding one vertex fixed at the seed kept a 60-generation trace on w031 (dominant share 0.972 within 4 voxels, 4.74 of 4.88 cm²) where the unanchored run from the same seed was dominant on w033 (0.511) with nothing on w031. One seed on one scroll, scored against the published windings; an anchor holds one point, not the sheet, and the anchor change is not public as code. [Log](logs/2026-10-12-upstream-reports.md).
14. **A traced surface's render comes out turned, and `ink_9um` reads it much worse until it is turned upright** (2026-10-13, model output on development data; the test's rule was fixed before the maps). Published segments are rendered with the scroll's axis down the rows, but a traced surface's render keeps whatever in-plane angle its grid has, and `ink_9um` was trained with right-angle turns only. On PHerc0841's published w00, a 30 degree turn cost `ink_9um` 0.155 AUC [0.077, 0.228], while a 90 degree turn cost 0.027 [-0.020, 0.078]. On one of my own traces of the same papyrus, whose render sat 49 degrees off, `ink_9um` scored 0.618 against 0.798 for the published map on the same 352,860 labeled pixels. Turning the render upright first, by an angle measured from the mesh alone, and the map back after, raised it by 0.256 [0.148, 0.368] to 0.874 (seed 42; seed 43 +0.181), and beat a sham turned 45 degrees past upright by 0.21 for both seeds. The same test with Reader v2 on 15 October did not replicate that cleanly: 0.851 as rendered, 0.911 upright, 0.872 for the sham, so upright minus sham was +0.039 [-0.005, 0.091], not clearly above zero, and the turn raised Reader v2's depth-reversed control from 0.476 to 0.709 (forward minus reversed fell from 0.375 to 0.202); `ink.orient` stays opt in ([fix4 release](https://github.com/ChaseHendrick/Scrolls/blob/submission/progress-2026-10-fix4/submissions/progress-2026-10-fix4/autopipeline/README.md)). One trace on one scroll, on pixels scored before, so not independent confirmation; the held-out test is still to come. AutoPipeline's `ink.orient` setting and `kit canvasorient` do the turn. [Log](logs/2026-10-14-upright-before-ink.md).

## Earlier measurements and corrections

1. **On PHerc0139 w045, which v8in never saw but `ink_9um` trained on through its 2.4 um render, v8in scores below `ink_9um`; this test cannot say which reads a surface neither saw better** (2026-10-07, corrected 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09). On PHerc0139 w045, which v8in never saw, v8in (YoussefMoNader/ink-8um-v8in, released 2026-09-28) reaches pixel AUC 0.738 (0.327 with depth reversed, the control) against 0.907 (0.384 reversed) for `ink_9um` inferred on the same bare 640 px crop. v8in's author reports validation during training on PHerc0009B segment ag132115 and two fragments (Frag3, Frag4), and released predictions on PHerc1447 ([model card](https://huggingface.co/YoussefMoNader/ink-8um-v8in), read 2026-10-09); TAUIL's benchmark and Reader v2's scoreboard do not include it. Correction (2026-10-08): w045 is not held out from `ink_9um`. Its 2.399 um render, pooled to 9.6 um, is one of `ink_9um`'s training segments (named pherc0139-w029 in the [label dataset](https://huggingface.co/buckets/scrollprize/datasets/tree/ink_9um)), and this test scores the native 9.362 um render of the same surface against that segment's human labels. So `ink_9um`'s 0.907 is a cross-scan score on a training surface, the test favors it more than first stated, and it cannot say whether v8in trails `ink_9um` on a surface neither saw. [Log](logs/2026-10-08-w045-not-held-out.md). Correction (2026-10-09): the first version set v8in's bare-crop map against 0.914, the same window cut from `ink_9um`'s whole-segment map, which is the mix finding 2 warns about; both `ink_9um` numbers (bare crop 0.9074, whole-segment window 0.9136) and v8in's 0.7382 are in [`docs/results.json`](results.json).
2. **Scoring a crop needs an edge margin** (2026-10-07). A map inferred on a bare 640 px crop differs from the same window of a whole-segment map by up to 0.03 AUC (PHerc0841 ag405: 0.821 against 0.793); leaving a 64 px edge out makes the two identical to four decimals on all three segments tested. Crop-based comparisons that skip this can rank models wrongly by that much.
3. **A threshold taken from a training segment misses about 96 % of real ink on an unseen scroll** (2026-10-07). The 0.7843 cut-off one published PHerc0826 null used is the median `ink_9um` value on labeled ink of PHerc0139 w035, a training segment. On PHerc0841's three labeled segments only 2.3 to 3.7 % of human-labeled ink reaches it (median 0.39 to 0.44); on w045, a surface `ink_9um` trained on through its 2.4 um render, 9.5 %. The same rule still finds about one candidate per cm² on PHerc0841, a third or fewer on labeled ink. Such nulls say little about faint ink. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-novel-checks.md).
4. **Averaging depth windows matches the best window chosen with labels, on an unseen scroll** (2026-10-07). The best 17-layer window moves by up to 4 layers between PHerc0841 segments and between readers (worth up to +0.085 AUC), and no label-free statistic we tried picks it reliably. The mean of four windows reaches the label-chosen optimum: 0.820 against 0.819 mean AUC over 8 reader-crop cases (0.785 with the default window). [Log](logs/2026-10-07-novel-checks.md) Related prior work, found later: window averaging of Hecate on PHerc0841 ([ArcheyChen](https://github.com/ArcheyChen/vesuvius-eligible-scouting)), a smaller window effect on whole PHerc0841 segments ([villa #1867](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/issues/1867), at most 0.023) and a negative result on training scrolls ([villa #1946](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/pull/1946)); what is ours is the comparison with the label-chosen window at 9 um ([log](logs/2026-10-07-overlap-and-baseline.md)).
5. **Two of PHerc0841's three labeled segments are the same papyrus** (2026-10-07). The published meshes of w00 and ag896 have median nearest-sampled-point distance 8.6 voxels (81 um) on a 47 um sampling grid, within 25 voxels over 98 % of their 12.7 cm², and where both are labeled their human ink labels agree (Dice 0.83, against 0.25 on average and at most 0.69 for 200 displaced matches); ag405 is a different surface (median gap 39 to 48 voxels). So PHerc0841 offers two independent labeled surfaces, not three: three-segment means count one sheet twice, and leave-one-segment-out between w00 and ag896 trains on the test sheet. Community reports had disagreed (different windings, or the same text); this measures it. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-overlap-and-baseline.md).
6. **Raw CT brightness alone carries almost no ink signal on PHerc0841** (2026-10-07). Each of the 28 surface-volume layers, used as an ink map, scores pixel AUC 0.43 to 0.58 on the crops and 0.47 to 0.54 on whole segments, and 0.49 to 0.51 after a 48 um high-pass; depth maximum and standard deviation reach at most 0.57. Readers reach 0.66 to 0.93 on the same crops, outperforming these brightness-only baselines. The 0.56 to 0.61 retained by `ink_9um` on depth-shuffled ag405 is also higher than those baselines. These comparisons do not isolate which features the models use or establish ink identity. Analogs exist on Paris 4 and fragments (at most 0.55 to 0.56); not found for PHerc0841. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-overlap-and-baseline.md).
7. **Ink-map agreement decreases with sampled-point distance between two traces** (2026-10-07, model output). On PHerc0841 w00/ag896, the team's maps correlate 0.86 within 28 um, 0.75 at 28 to 56 um, 0.45 at 56 to 84 um and 0.07 at 112 to 169 um. Thick-slab CT still correlates about 0.4 to 0.5 at the larger distances. These measurements use a 47 um point grid, so distance includes lateral sampling error. They do not isolate normal displacement, distance from the ink layer or a universal tracing tolerance. Whether a bounded correction restores common-label accuracy needs a controlled test. The team's maps are its published `new_canon_autoresearch_recipe` maps, and that recipe's committed trainer lists w00, ag896 and ag405 as training fragments, so these maps are probably in-sample on PHerc0841 (Interpretation; which checkpoint rendered them is not verified; [log](logs/2026-10-09-pherc0841-hecate.md)). [Log](logs/2026-10-07-overlap-and-baseline.md).
8. **PHerc0841 has limited supervision, and reported AUCs need uncertainty** (2026-10-07, measurement). Supervision totals 3.4 cm² across the three segments (1.41, 0.86 and 1.12 cm²; 0.84 cm² of ink summed), covering 7 to 11 % of each. Overlap has not been deduplicated, and w00/ag896 share one sheet. A 1 mm block bootstrap on the team's published maps gives 95 % interval half-widths of 0.008 (w00) to 0.038 (ag405). Those maps come from the `new_canon_autoresearch_recipe`, whose committed trainer ([villa 94602c91](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/blob/94602c91/ink-detection/train_resnet3d_3d_decoder.py), lines 200-207 and 292-293) lists w00, ag896 and ag405 as training fragments, so they are probably in-sample here (Interpretation; [log](logs/2026-10-09-pherc0841-hecate.md)); these intervals describe that scoring setup, not a universal noise floor for model rankings. Reader differences need paired comparisons on common pixels and an independent-surface check. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-overlap-and-baseline.md).
9. **Cross-trace agreement suggests a label-free triage experiment** (2026-10-07, model output and untested idea). On PHerc0841, a spot in the top 20 % of one ink map also lies in the other's top 20 % about 70 % of the time within 28 um, compared with 50 % for letter-scale CT texture and a 20 % independent-match expectation. The margin decreases with trace separation. Using this agreement to flag target surfaces is an untested hypothesis, requiring preregistration and held-out false-positive and sensitivity checks. Disagreement alone does not identify which trace is correctly placed or justify a correction. Related geometry and window studies are cited in the log; no claim of priority for agreement-based filtering is established. The maps compared are the team's, probably in-sample on PHerc0841 (see finding 8), so how far this agreement holds on a surface their model never trained on is untested. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-overlap-and-baseline.md).
10. **Every published surface volume now matches a published mesh, but one stored cross-scan matrix contradicts its own landmarks** (2026-10-07, measurement on public geometry, no ink). All 692 surface volumes in the bucket catalog reproduce their canvas exactly from a mesh variant naming the rendered volume, closing the gap reported in [villa #1727](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/issues/1727). Of 29 stored transforms, PHerc1667 1.129 um to 2.399 um misses its six landmarks by 42 to 212 um, while a refit of the same landmarks fits them within 4 um. Transforms into the 2023 7.91 um scans imply scales 0.7 to 1.5 % above the nominal voxel ratio (Paris 4, PHerc0332, PHerc1667), which suggests those voxels are about 1 % smaller than stated. Cross meshes are exact affine images of the traced mesh, and where measurable they put the papyrus within a few micrometers of the native render. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-mesh-hypothesis.md), [data](data/mesh-hypothesis/README.md). Related prior work, found later: kadenpool reported the PHerc1667 matrix in [villa #1843](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/issues/1843) (20 September 2026) with a refit. Update 2026-10-12: a check in the CT itself agrees with the landmarks, not the matrix. Cubes around the six landmarks, carried through the published matrix, sit 42 to 216 um from where the 2.399 um scan matches them, within a few micrometers of the landmark residuals, and the landmarks sit 3.3 to 5.7 um from the CT's match; swapped landmark pairs match 0 of 6, and two other stored transforms pass the same check. Posted as a [comment on villa #1843](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/issues/1843#issuecomment-6130119937) ([log](logs/2026-10-12-upstream-reports.md)).
11. **PHerc1667's published 1.129 um renders appear misplaced, and 1.129 um renders on three other scrolls sit about 16 um from their 2.4 um renders** (2026-10-07, measurement; the PHerc1667 result is post hoc). Phase correlation of center layers on 3 mm tiles: on three control scrolls the 1.129 um renders are offset by a constant (-11.9, -11.0) um from the 2.4 um renders (216 tiles, spread 1.7 um). On five PHerc1667 segments, tiles with clear matches follow the shift predicted if the landmarks are right and the matrix wrong (median miss 7.7 um, slope 0.93), and matches vanish where that predicted displacement is large (median 158 um, up to 475 um). The pre-registered version of this test failed its own control bar, so this needs a calibrated rerun before anyone relies on it. [Log](logs/2026-10-07-mesh-hypothesis.md).
