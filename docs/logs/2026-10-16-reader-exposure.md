# Which released reader trained on which labelled segment (2026-10-16)

**Question.** PHerc0841 (w00, ag896, ag405) is this repository's held-out test, but since 9 October it has said the scroll is "possibly not held out from Hecate" ([log](2026-10-09-pherc0841-hecate.md)). Settle it from public sources, and for PHerc0841, PHerc0139 w045 and w043, and PHerc1447 w058 and w060 say which released reader trained on each. Nothing was rerun on a GPU, and nobody was asked.

**Answer.** Hecate is **not** a held-out reader on PHerc0841 (Interpretation, from the Sourced and Community lines below), but the exposure is not where the 9 October log first looked. Hecate's own training stages, as published, would never draw a PHerc0841 training patch: every supervised pixel of the three PHerc0841 label sets lies inside the dataset's validation mask, so the published split rule gives each segment zero training patches (Sourced fact for the code and masks; our computation; that the released run used this rule's split is Interpretation, item 1 below). The exposure comes through Hecate's starting weights (`ink_canonical_2um`, whose committed trainer lists all three segments as training fragments). Two weaker channels may add to it: checkpoint selection on a validation score that would average PHerc0841 in, and its Paris 4 3D teacher, whose first stage trained on eight scrolls' labels including PHerc0841 but which only made targets for Paris 4, so it is indirect at most. Team members also said on the community Discord in September 2026 that Hecate's training used almost all of the available ink labels and that the released label sets had been at least partly used to train the published models (Community reports, paraphrased). So Hecate's PHerc0841 numbers, and the team's `new_canon_autoresearch_recipe` maps there, stay possibly in-sample; every other released reader's PHerc0841 status is unchanged (held out).

Further facts for the other segments. **w043**, which `kit/autobatch.py`'s comment calls "held-out" when it picks Hecate 2.4 um for fine scans, is in the training lists of `ink_9um`, d9v2 and Reader v2, and very likely in Hecate's: it has no validation mask in Hecate's dataset, and the published rule puts 74.5 % of its supervised pixels under training patches (68.2 % to 93.7 % over seeds 0 to 39). It is **not** in the canonical model's committed fragment list. **w045** is in the canonical list, and the published Hecate rule puts 86.8 % of its supervised pixels under training patches. PHerc1447 is in neither Hecate's dataset nor the canonical list.

Labels follow [`../NOVELTY.md`](../NOVELTY.md). This log supersedes the held-out wording of the [2026-10-09 log](2026-10-09-pherc0841-hecate.md), which stays as a record.

## Hecate, channel by channel

1. **Its own stages.** The Hecate trainer the team published (villa branch `distilled_3d_ink`, 5ff00ca0, S4) builds its split from the ink dataset the Hecate card cites (S1, S5). For each segment it uses the validation mask as the holdout when it has any pixel (`multiteacher.py:186-195`), keeps every 256 px training patch at least 256 px clear of that holdout (`:111-134`), and records `validation_only` when no training patch is left (`:214`). Training draws come only from segments with training patches (`:285-293`). For PHerc0841 the three `validation_mask_v2` files contain every supervised pixel (M1), so all three are validation-only. Both trainers in that branch (the multi-teacher one with human 2D supervision, and the resolution distillation that produced the 9.6 um model from the 2.4 um one) read the same manifest. Limit: the trainer's guide says to use "the original audited manifest" and that a new one "creates a new split, not a reproduction of an archived run" (`vesuvius/docs/hecate.md:19`); that manifest is not public, so that the released run used this split is Interpretation. Two community checks of the open-data copies of these masks found the same 100 % (S13).
2. **Its starting weights.** The card: "We started from the canonical 2 µm ink detector" and, for the 2.4 um model, "We preserved as much of the canonical model's architecture and pretrained weights as possible" (S1). The canonical checkpoint's committed trainer lists w00, ag405 and ag896 in `TRAIN_FRAGMENT_IDS` (lines 200-207), reads them from `2um_dataset/841_2um` (lines 292-293), holds out only `l5_0_crop` (line 794) and reads no validation mask (tile loop, lines 384-418) (S3). One caveat: that loop keeps a tile only where the segment's own `{id}_mask` file is nonzero over the whole tile (line 413, loaded by `train_resnet3d_lib/data_ops.read_image_mask`, lines 178-185, in the same commit), and those files in the team's `2um_dataset` are not public, so whether they cut anything out of the PHerc0841 segments is unknown. Its checkpoint name, epoch 13 and 8,089 steps per epoch (113,246 total) match the released file (S2, S3). Whether the 9.6 um student started from canonical weights is not stated (the guide says student weights are random unless a checkpoint is given, `hecate.md:36`); its targets came from the 2.4 um model (S1), so it inherits that model's exposure either way (Interpretation).
3. **Its teachers (weak, indirect).** The Paris 4 3D teacher's card says it was trained in stages, the first a "teacher on 8-scroll surface-conditioned ink labels (PHerc. Paris 4, 0139, 0500P2, 0814, 0841, 1667, MAN5, 9B)"; its final config trains on Paris 4 only (S6). Hecate used it for Paris 4 3D targets, and the trainer gives Paris 4 segments a teacher index of their own (`teacher_id` 0, every other scroll 1; `data/multiteacher.py:208`), so Hecate never saw PHerc0841 CT through this teacher. At most, weights shaped in part by PHerc0841 labels shaped Hecate's Paris 4 targets: an indirect, weak channel (Interpretation). Whether that first stage held PHerc0841's validation mask out is not stated.
4. **Checkpoint choice (weak).** The multi-teacher trainer keeps `best.pth` by `val/macro/pr_auc` (`training/multiteacher.py:543, 613`), a mean over scrolls' validation patches, which would include PHerc0841's. The card says only "We then froze selected student snapshots" (S1). Selecting on PHerc0841 validation is a weak exposure, if it happened (Interpretation).
5. **What the team said.** Asked on 25 September whether PHerc0841 was held out from `hecate_9.6um`, a team member answered that Hecate's training used almost all of the available ink labels, and advised making new segmentations to test on material a model has not seen (S12). On 28 September, in a community thread about the PHerc0841 validation masks, a team member advised mostly ignoring the validation set and said the label sets were published as a first batch that had been at least partly used to train the published models (S13). Community reports, paraphrased (team members on the community Discord, not a training record).

## Measurement M1: the published split rule on the published masks

It read the masks below from the ink dataset (kept outside git), CPU only, a few minutes in all. ## Not verified, and what would settle it

- Which fragment list, and which per-segment `_mask` files, the run behind the released `ink_canonical_2um` used, and the archived Hecate manifest. Only the authors can say; asking them is an outward step and needs Chase's go-ahead.
- How the 9.6 um student was initialized, and whether the Paris 4 teacher's first stage held PHerc0841's validation mask out.
- The w045 crop's position relative to Hecate's training patches (it sits on another grid; not mapped).
- Indirect exposure of d9v2 and Reader v2 through the team's dense targets (unchanged from the 2026-10-09 log; Untested idea).
- Untested idea: score Hecate and canonical 2um on w043 separately on Hecate's two generated holdout blocks (above) and on the pixels under its training patches.
