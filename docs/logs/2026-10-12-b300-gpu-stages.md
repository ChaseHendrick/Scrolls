# Where B300 GPUs would help villa users (12 October 2026)

**Chase Hendrick**, Independent Researcher · [ORCID 0009-0002-9754-6087](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-9754-6087)

Research note, not a claim about any scroll. Asked for by the owner on 12 October 2026. Labels: **Sourced fact** and **Untested idea** as in [`../NOVELTY.md`](../NOVELTY.md), plus **Measured** (our run; its record is in our private repository) and **Interpretation** (our arithmetic or reading). villa paths are at commit 4eecd08 (main, 12 October 2026) unless a line says otherwise. Prices are Modal list prices read on 12 October 2026 ([pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)); [`../compute/modal-pricing.md`](../compute/modal-pricing.md) has the rest.

## Short answer

- **Measured on villa's surface model.** m7 through `vesuvius.predict` at the catalog's settings, 512 patches of one PHerc0841 box read from local disk: 0.039 s per patch on a B300, 0.052 on an H100 and 0.309 on an L4. That makes the B300 1.3 times as fast as an H100 and 7.9 times as fast as an L4, and all three maps matched the published one (Dice 0.9998) (Measured, below).
- **Per patch, the H100 is cheapest.** A B300 costs 7.10 USD/h on Modal, an H100 3.95 and an L4 0.80 (Sourced fact). With 16 cores and 64 GiB per container that is about 9.1 USD per 100,000 patches on the B300, 7.6 on the H100 and 17.7 on the L4 (Interpretation). A B300 pays off only when wall time matters more than cost.
- **Reading from S3 throws the speed away.** Read straight from the bucket, the B300 was 5.6 times slower per patch than from local disk at the same batch, and slower than an L4 reading from disk; its GPU was busy about 7 % of the time. From local disk the GPU was busy most of the time once patches were flowing (roughly 90 % on the B300 at batch 4), so there a B300 is simply about 1.3 times an H100 on this network. Batch 16 was slower than batch 4 on both fast cards (Measured, with our estimate of GPU use after start-up, below).
- **Ink training on a B300 waited on its batch loader** (Measured, below).
- **The best community target is still surface prediction for scans that have none.** On 9 October 2026 the open-data catalog had a surface prediction and normal grids for 41 of its 74 volumes, and none for any 7.91 µm scan (Measured: counted from the catalog cache and bucket listings on 9 October 2026; the catalog may have added predictions since). VC3D's seed-growing tracer (`vc_grow_seg_from_seed`) starts from that prediction and its normal grids. The catalog ran m7 only at level 0 of 8.64 to 9.366 µm scans and level 2 of 2.2 to 2.4 µm scans, so a 7.91 µm scan needs a check first. At the measured rates, 100,000 patches take about 1.1 h on a B300, 1.5 h on an H100 and 8.6 h on an L4, and `vesuvius.predict --num_parts` splits a scan across cards (Interpretation).
- **Other stages that need a fast card:** the 256³ fiber and surface models trained with `vesuvius.train`, training ink models, native 3D ink inference at full resolution, and the long spiral-fitting and dinovol runs. None of these is measured here.
- **No gain:** tracing (GrowPatch), rendering and flattening are CPU work.

## Setup on a B300

- B300 requires CUDA 13.1 or newer (Sourced fact, [Modal GPU guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/gpu)). A PyTorch build for CUDA 12 (cu128 and older) does not run there. PyTorch 2.14.1+cu132 under Python 3.14 ran ink-model training and `vesuvius.predict` on a B300 on 12 October 2026 (Measured: card reported as "NVIDIA B300 SXM6 AC", CUDA 13.2). The same image ran unchanged on an H100 and an L4.
- `vesuvius` already targets CUDA 13: `requires-python >=3.14,<3.15`, `torch>=2.12,<3`, `cucim-cu13` from NVIDIA's index (Sourced fact, `vesuvius/pyproject.toml` lines 9, 50, 92, 253-259). Installing it with `--extra-index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu132` gives a B300-ready environment. The image we use: `debian_slim` with Python 3.12, `uv python install 3.14`, villa cloned and pinned, then `uv pip install --index-strategy unsafe-best-match --extra-index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu132 -e villa/vesuvius` plus the `models` extra (without `volume-cartographer`, `cucim`, `nnunetv2` and `batchgeneratorsv2`), and villa's vendored `segmentation/models/arch/nnunet` and `batchgeneratorsv2` installed editable. Build it with an assert that `torch.version.cuda` is 13.1 or newer, so a CUDA 12 wheel fails at build time, not on the card.
- Still pinned to CUDA 12, so they need their own environment or a port first (Sourced fact):
  - `spiral-fitting/pyproject.toml` lines 7-62: torch `>=2.11,<2.13` from the cu128 index, `cupy-cuda12x`, `cucim-cu12`, `brook-cu12`.
  - `ink-detection/optimized_inference/Dockerfile` line 3: `pytorch/pytorch:2.10.0-cuda12.8-cudnn9-runtime`.
- VC3D's optional ECL-MaxFlow build (`-DVC_ENABLE_ECL_MAXFLOW=ON`) sets `CMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES 70` when none is given (`volume-cartographer/CMakeLists.txt` lines 77-91, Sourced fact). CUDA 13's nvcc no longer builds sm_70 (Interpretation, from NVIDIA's CUDA 13 release notes), so pass the card's architecture.
- lasagna compiles its CUDA extensions at first use through `torch.utils.cpp_extension.load` (`lasagna/grid_sample_3d_u8.py` lines 6-20), so the image needs an nvcc that matches the torch build. Its installer offers cu130 (CUDA 13.0) with torch 2.13.0 (`lasagna/scripts/bootstrap_venv.py` lines 37-42), below the 13.1 that Modal names for a B300; a cu132 build would need adding and is untested.
- Modal: request `gpu="B300"` (or a list with fallbacks) and leave `region` unset (Sourced fact, [GPU guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/gpu), [region selection](https://modal.com/docs/guide/region-selection)). Stage the input on a CPU container into a Volume and copy it to the GPU container's local disk before predicting (Interpretation, from the S3 and local runs below).

## Measured so far

**m7 on a B300, an H100 and an L4** (12 October 2026, Modal, 2.14 USD in all). The same box as test A below, read once from the bucket with one patch (192 voxels) of margin on every side and copied to each container's local disk. villa e0bbb8b, PyTorch 2.14.1+cu132, CUDA 13.2 on every card; batch 4, no TTA, the catalog's normalization, 16 loader workers, `nnUNet_compile=0`; 16 cores and 64 GiB per container. Seconds per patch = (512-patch run less the 16-patch run at batch 4 from local disk) / 496, which takes out model loading and start-up. The batch-16 and S3 runs had no short run of their own, so their figures assume the same start-up. One run per setting.

| Card | Batch 4: s per patch | Batch 4: wall s for 512 | Batch 4: mean GPU use, whole run | Batch 16: s per patch (GPU use) | USD per 100,000 patches (container) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| B300 | 0.0391 | 47.9 | 38 % | 0.0683 (19 %) | 9.1 |
| H100 (pinned, no H200 upgrade) | 0.0522 | 49.1 | 45 % | 0.0625 (38 %) | 7.6 |
| L4 | 0.3089 | 189.4 | 80 % | not run | 17.7 |

- GPU use is the mean of nvidia-smi samples about once a second over the whole `vesuvius.predict` process, start-up included. Start-up was 59 % of the B300's batch-4 run and 19 % of the L4's. Leaving out the samples of the 16-patch run gives roughly 90 % on the B300, 81 % on the H100 and 96 % on the L4 while patches were flowing, and about 33 % for the B300 at batch 16 (Interpretation: a rough estimate from 17 to 146 one-second samples).
- The B300 reading straight from S3 at batch 16: 0.3853 s per patch at 6 % mean GPU use (about 7 % after start-up), 5.6 times slower than the same run from local disk. In wall time it took 219.6 s against 62.4 s.
- Why batch 16 was slower than batch 4 on both fast cards is not known; it was not investigated.
- Every card's map against the published `m7-L0-th0.2` map: Dice 0.9998 (0.9998 inside a 32-voxel margin), 0.9999 between any two cards. Moving the map 4 voxels in x drops Dice to 0.036, and comparing one half of the box in z with the other gives 0.185, so the check can fail.
- Start-up (model load to first patches) was longer on the B300 than on the H100 (28.5 s against 23.2 s), so on this small box the two finished 1.2 s apart. On a whole scan start-up is paid once.
- GPU-only prices give 7.7 USD per 100,000 patches on the B300, 5.7 on the H100 and 6.9 on the L4; the L4 used most of its GPU and would need fewer cores than it was given (Interpretation).
- The script also tried to time the network's forward pass alone, with no data loading. That failed on every card (`ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'nnunet'` while loading the checkpoint outside `vesuvius.predict`) and was not retried, so the GPU-alone ceiling, and how much of the 0.039 s is the network, are not measured.
- A whole 9.366 µm PHerc0841 scan is up to about 1.3 million grid patches before empty ones are skipped, (19400/96) × (7703/96)² (Interpretation). At the rates above, all of them would take about 14 h on one B300 (about 120 USD), 19 h on one H100 (about 100 USD) or 110 h on one L4 (about 230 USD); the scroll fills only part of that grid.

**m7 on an L4, from S3** (surface prediction test A, 9 October 2026): PHerc0841 `20250821151531` 9.366 µm, level 0, box `9408:9984,3648:4224,3648:4224`, villa e0bbb8b, batch 4, no TTA, 8 loader workers, input read from S3. 512 patches in 277 s, 53.3 % mean GPU use, 21.2 of 22.5 GiB. Dice 0.9924 against the published `m7-L0-th0.2` map; moving our map 4 voxels in x drops it to 0.036, so the check can fail. That is about 0.54 s per patch end to end, or about 21.5 USD per 100,000 patches with 8 cores and 32 GiB (Interpretation).

**Ink training step on a B300** (12 October 2026; a hybrid 3D-to-2D ink model of the `ink_9um` architecture, started from Reader v2 (a community fine-tune of `ink_9um`), 17 × 128 × 128 crops, AdamW, EMA; no scores were taken):

| Measurement | Batch 16 | Batch 64 | Batch 256 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Training step alone (frozen anchor network run once), patches/s | 428 | 685 | 812 |
| Real training with the batch loader, patches/s | 244 (22 threads) | 527 (44 threads) | not run |
| Share of the real step not explained by the GPU step (1 - GPU-only / real) | 43 % | 23 % | |

- Peak memory at batch 256: 26 GiB of the card's memory.
- Two, four or eight trainings on one B300 at once gave 402 patches/s in all, against 424 for one: packing runs on one card gains nothing.
- The loader (40 vCPUs) topped out near 39 batches/s of 16 with threads (at 44 threads) and 52 with 16 processes; more processes past 16 made it slower (26.5 at 44), and more threads changed little.
- With the frozen network run six times inside each step instead of once, the step fell from 768 to 345 patches/s at batch 128.

Interpretation: on a B300, run one training per card at a large batch, and spend the effort on the loader (processes rather than threads, data staged on local disk). For surface prediction, stage the scan on local disk; read from S3, a faster read path is what would let a B300 earn its price. villa #2009 (chunk-local patch order for `vesuvius.predict` with `--chunk_cache_mb`) should matter more on a fast card than on a slow one, and #2003 (shared chunk reads) does the same for ink inference (Untested idea: we have not timed them).
