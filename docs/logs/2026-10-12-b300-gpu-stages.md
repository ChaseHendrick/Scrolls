# Where B300 GPUs would help villa users (12 October 2026)

**Chase Hendrick**, Independent Researcher · [ORCID 0009-0002-9754-6087](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-9754-6087)

Research note, not a claim about any scroll. Asked for by the owner on 12 October 2026. Labels follow [`../NOVELTY.md`](../NOVELTY.md): **Sourced fact**, **Measured** (our run, with its record), **Interpretation** (our arithmetic), **Untested idea**. villa paths are at commit 4eecd08 (main, 12 October 2026) unless a line says otherwise. Prices are Modal list prices read on 12 October 2026 ([pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)); [`../compute/modal-pricing.md`](../compute/modal-pricing.md) has the rest.

## Short answer

- A B300 costs 7.10 USD/h on Modal, an H100 3.95 and an L4 0.80 (Sourced fact). It must be about 1.8 times as fast as an H100 to cost less per patch (Interpretation).
- The stages where a fast card matters are the big 3D networks: surface prediction (m7 and the fiber and normal nnU-Nets), training ink and surface models, native 3D ink inference at full resolution, and the long spiral-fitting and dinovol runs.
- The best single target is **surface prediction for scans that have none**: on 9 October 2026 the open-data catalog had a surface prediction and normal grids for 41 of its 74 volumes, and none for any 7.91 µm scan (Measured: counted from the catalog cache and bucket listings on 9 October 2026; the catalog may have added predictions since). Every tracer starts from that prediction.
- Tracing (GrowPatch), rendering and flattening are CPU work. A B300 does nothing for them.
- At B300 speed the data loader, not the GPU, becomes the limit. Measured on an ink training step below.

## Setup on a B300

- B300 requires CUDA 13.1 or newer (Sourced fact, [Modal GPU guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/gpu)). A PyTorch build for CUDA 12 (cu128 and older) does not run there. PyTorch 2.14.1+cu132 under Python 3.14 ran ink-model training on a B300 on 12 October 2026 (Measured: card reported as "NVIDIA B300 SXM6 AC", CUDA 13.2).
- `vesuvius` already targets CUDA 13: `requires-python >=3.14,<3.15`, `torch>=2.12,<3`, `cucim-cu13` from NVIDIA's index (Sourced fact, `vesuvius/pyproject.toml` lines 9, 50, 92, 253-259). Installing it with `--extra-index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu132` gives a B300-ready environment. The image we use: `debian_slim` with Python 3.12, `uv python install 3.14`, villa cloned and pinned, then `uv pip install --index-strategy unsafe-best-match --extra-index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu132 -e villa/vesuvius` plus the `models` extra (without `volume-cartographer`, `cucim`, `nnunetv2` and `batchgeneratorsv2`), and villa's vendored `segmentation/models/arch/nnunet` and `batchgeneratorsv2` installed editable. Build it with an assert that `torch.version.cuda` is 13.1 or newer, so a CUDA 12 wheel fails at build time, not on the card.
- Still pinned to CUDA 12, so they need their own environment or a port first (Sourced fact):
  - `spiral-fitting/pyproject.toml` lines 7-62: torch `>=2.11,<2.13` from the cu128 index, `cupy-cuda12x`, `cucim-cu12`, `brook-cu12`.
  - `ink-detection/optimized_inference/Dockerfile` line 3: `pytorch/pytorch:2.10.0-cuda12.8-cudnn9-runtime`.
  - VC3D's optional CUDA maxflow: `CMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES 70` by default (`volume-cartographer/CMakeLists.txt` lines 77-91). CUDA 13's nvcc no longer builds sm_70 (Interpretation, from NVIDIA's CUDA 13 release notes), so set it to the card's architecture.
- lasagna compiles its CUDA extensions at first use through `torch.utils.cpp_extension.load` (`lasagna/grid_sample_3d_u8.py` lines 6-20), so the image needs an nvcc that matches the torch build. Its installer already offers cu130 with torch 2.13.0 (`lasagna/scripts/bootstrap_venv.py` lines 30-55).
- Modal: request `gpu="B300"` (or a list with fallbacks), stage data on a CPU container into a Volume before attaching the GPU, and leave `region` unset (Sourced fact, [GPU guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/gpu), [region selection](https://modal.com/docs/guide/region-selection)).

## Stage by stage

| Stage (villa) | What a person waits on today | B300 effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface prediction, m7 nnU-Net, 192³ patches (`vesuvius.predict`) | No published timing for a whole scan. One 576³ box of PHerc0841 (512 patches) took 277 s on an L4 read from S3, at 53 % mean GPU use (Measured, below) | Large, if the input is staged locally. B300 timing pending (below) |
| Fiber and normal nnU-Nets, 256³ patches | Trained as 8-GPU DDP (`vesuvius/src/vesuvius/models/configuration/single_task/ps256_fibers_dicece.yaml` lines 14-20); full 256³ inference ran out of memory on an RTX 4090 (`vesuvius/docs/guided_dinovol.md` line 100) | 288 GB per card (Interpretation: Modal's 2,304 GB for 8) fits 256³ inference and larger batches |
| Ink training, 9 µm hybrid recipe | About 5 h on one H100; native 3D at level 2 about 10 h ([tutorial](https://scrollprize.org/tutorial5), Sourced fact) | GPU step fast; loader-bound (Measured, below) |
| Native 3D ink inference | About 78,000 patches and 2.5 h on an H100 at level 2 for the tutorial segment; "hundreds of thousands" at full resolution (tutorial, Sourced fact) | The inference job big enough to need a fast card (Interpretation) |
| 2.5D 9 µm ink inference | An M1 Pro runs `ink_9um` over a 40 cm² segment in about 11 min per pass ([`../mac.md`](../mac.md), Measured) | None worth paying for |
| Spiral fitting | Full z range "needs a lot of GPU memory and time" (`scrollprize.org/docs/38_tutorial_spiral.md` line 151); surface tracks for z 10752-14848 in 40 s on one H100, 16 to 20 s on four (`spiral-fitting/README.md` lines 876-882) | Memory, once ported to CUDA 13. Not measured |
| dinovol pretraining | Released checkpoints at 352,500 steps (`scrollprize.org/docs/37_2026_open_problems.md` line 576); no hours published | Long runs; not measured |
| Neural tracing models | `remote_l0_fiber_ft` about 31 GB and 1.4 s per iteration on one H100 (`vesuvius/src/vesuvius/neural_tracing/tracers/sheets/OPERATIONS.md` lines 139-140) | Not measured |
| GrowPatch, render, flatten | CPU by design | None. `volume-cartographer/core/src/GrowPatch.cpp` line 3009: "currently CPU seems always faster (40x , AMD 5800H vs RTX 3080 mobile...)" |

## Measured so far

**m7 on an L4** (surface prediction test A, 9 October 2026): PHerc0841 `20250821151531` 9.366 µm, level 0, box `9408:9984,3648:4224,3648:4224`, villa e0bbb8b, batch 4, no TTA, 8 loader workers, input read from S3. 512 patches in 277 s, 53.3 % mean GPU use, 21.7 of 23.0 GiB. Dice 0.9924 against the published `m7-L0-th0.2` map; moving our map 4 voxels in x drops it to 0.036, so the check can fail. That is about 0.54 s per patch end to end, or about 21 USD per 100,000 patches with 8 cores and 32 GiB (Interpretation).

**Ink training step on a B300** (12 October 2026; a hybrid 3D-to-2D ink model fine-tuned from `ink_9um`, 17 × 128 × 128 crops, AdamW, EMA; no scores were taken):

| Measurement | Batch 16 | Batch 64 | Batch 256 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Training step alone, patches/s | 428 | 685 | 812 |
| Real training with the batch loader, patches/s | 244 (22 threads) | 527 (44 threads) | not run |
| Share of time waiting for data | 43 % | 23 % | |

- Peak memory at batch 256: 26 GiB of the card's memory.
- Two, four or eight trainings on one B300 at once gave 402 patches/s in all, against 424 for one: packing runs on one card gains nothing.
- The loader (40 vCPUs) topped out near 39 batches/s of 16 with threads and 52 with 16 processes; more workers past 16 made it slower.
- With six frozen teacher networks run inside each step, the step fell to 345 patches/s at batch 128.

Interpretation: on a B300, run one training per card at a large batch, and spend the effort on the loader (processes rather than threads, data staged on local disk). Recent villa PRs that cut reads, such as #2003 (shared chunk reads) and #2009 (chunk-local patch order for `vesuvius.predict`), are worth more on a fast card than on a slow one.
