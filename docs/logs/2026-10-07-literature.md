## What changes our plan

1. **Surface morphology needs about 1 µm; at 9 µm it is mostly gone.** Angelotti, Nicolardi, Henderson, Seales, [arXiv:2603.27698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27698) (29 Mar 2026; [Scientific Reports 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-58467-1)). On profilometry of opened papyri (PHerc. 248, 250, 500P2), 2D nnU-Nets separate ink from papyrus by surface relief alone: median Dice 0.890 at 0.34 µm, 0.754 at 1.02 µm, 0.516 at 2.04 µm, about 0.03 at 3.4 µm; a model trained at 10.88 µm reaches 0.467. Leave-one-papyrus-out: 0.757, 0.817 and **0.475**, so transfer between papyri is uneven even at the finest scale. (Sourced fact) **For us** (Interpretation): whatever 9 µm readers detect is not fine relief, and transfer varies by manuscript, which is what PHerc0841 already shows. For training idea 2 (a 1 µm teacher), a 9 µm student can only learn what survives at 9 µm; the teacher's sharper maps may not help as much as hoped, so measure it, do not assume it.
2. **How PHerc. 1667 was read: per-scroll pseudo-labelling and hand-corrected meshes.** [arXiv:2606.29085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29085) (25 Jun 2026). A fragment-trained 3D-ResNet-encoder, 2D-decoder model (trained on PHerc. 9B, 343P, 500P2 infrared images), run at 2.4 µm with a 256 px window (about 614 µm, deliberately smaller than a letter so it cannot draw letters it has not seen), then **five rounds of scroll-specific pseudo-labelling**; a 1.1 µm region scan for illegible parts; meshes from an nnU-Net surface model, grown automatically and **corrected by hand** where compression caused mergers and sheet switches; eight papyrologists endorsed readings column by column. The authors: "ink segmentation remains weak, varies across ink recipes and local degradation states, and is further modulated by scan conditions." (Sourced fact) **For us** (Untested idea): a fourth training idea, self-training on a target scroll's own pseudo-labels, is how the team got legibility; it is risky without labels on the target (it can amplify a model's own artifacts) and would need the PHerc0841 test to show it helps an unseen scroll first. It also confirms the roadmap's Phase 2: hand correction was essential for the one scroll read end to end.
3. **Lead in some inks makes letters bright in CT, and XRF can screen for it.** Seiler et al., *PLOS One* 21(9): e0353485 (Sep 2026; [coverage](https://archaeologymag.com/2026/09/scientists-burn-their-scrolls-to-read-herculaneum-papyri/)). Model scrolls written with lampblack ink spiked with lead, carbonized, then scanned: leaded letters were far brighter than papyrus (down to about 25 µg/cm² of lead, up to 25 times the X-ray absorption). They propose handheld XRF screening to choose which scrolls to scan. (Sourced fact) **For us** (Interpretation): we cannot screen scrolls physically, but "ink recipe" is a real variable; if any eligible scroll's ink is leaded, ink would show as density in the raw CT before any model. A cheap check on our side: look for unusually bright, letter-sized thin features on rendered surfaces.

## Methods and tools worth knowing

- **Diffeomorphic spiral fitting.** Henderson, [arXiv:2512.04927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04927) (4 Dec 2025); code [pmh47/spiral-fitting](https://github.com/pmh47/spiral-fitting), the basis of villa's `spiral-fitting/`. Fits one deformed Archimedean spiral to surface predictions, guaranteeing a single sheet without self-intersections; beats ThaumatoAnakalyptor on PHerc. Paris 4 (chamfer 5.29 vs 5.59). Limitations stated: it can wander between windings where predictions conflict, and struggles in compressed or broken regions. This is where the atlas meshes come from (pscamillo's spiral fits). (Sourced fact)
- **Unsupervised volumetric texture ink detection.** Korotetskyi and Haindl, [Zenodo 20766169](https://zenodo.org/records/20766169) (19 Jun 2026, CC BY 4.0, code and data in a 277 MB archive). First-order statistics, Laws' texture energy and block autocorrelation in 3D, fused and smoothed: mean MCC 0.374 on 25 annotated letters of PHerc. 172, positive on every specimen. (Sourced fact) **For us** (Untested idea): no training data means no training-scroll bias; as a second, model-free reader it could flag regions where a learned model and a texture statistic agree. Untested at 9 µm.
- **Kaggle Surface Detection (closed March 2026, 200,000 USD).** First place: an nnU-Net ensemble with post-processing ([writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/c/vesuvius-challenge-surface-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution-for-the-vesuvius-challenge-su)); others used skeleton-recall loss and Frangi vesselness filtering to keep sheets continuous ([7th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/vesuvius-challenge-surface-detection/writeups/7st-place-solution-for-the-vesuvius-challenge)). These shaped the team's surface model that GrowPatch and spiral fits start from. (Sourced fact)
- **Volumetric Fast Fourier Convolution for ink.** [arXiv:2308.05070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05070) (2023): an earlier ink architecture; context only.
- **EduceLab-Scrolls.** [arXiv:2304.02084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02084) (2023): the aligned fragment dataset (infrared photographs aligned to CT) that made supervised ink detection possible.

## Sweep, 3 November 2026 (window 31 Oct to 3 Nov: arXiv and Zenodo)

Searched arXiv and Zenodo for 31 October to 3 November 2026. arXiv coverage ends 2 November, because 3 November submissions were not yet announced. Nothing in the window covered Herculaneum, the Vesuvius Challenge, papyrus CT, carbonized scrolls, ink detection from CT, or Greek handwriting recognition. Each URL and date below was checked on 3 Nov; nothing was run. Entries are in [`../papers.json`](../papers.json).

1. **ContactCut** (Ko, Jeong, Wang; [arXiv:2611.01457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01457), v1 1 Nov 2026; code https://github.com/bentake/ContactCut, license not stated). Sourced fact: it corrects a flagged false merge near one localized cue point. It picks native contacts as source and sink seeds on the supervoxel contact graph, runs a fixed seeded solver (graph cut, random walker or watershed) and ranks the whole 3D partitions that result. Inference uses only the pre-edit graph and the cue, with no raw volume. Evaluated on MICrONS and FlyWire. Untested idea: splitting merged wraps where two sheets touch, from one cue point.
2. **Kiln v0.6.0 and v0.6.1** (Martin Panknin; [Zenodo 23832534](https://zenodo.org/records/23832534) for v0.6.1 and [Zenodo 23831742](https://zenodo.org/records/23831742) for v0.6.0, both 31 Oct 2026, Apache-2.0; code https://github.com/kilnrender/kiln-render). Sourced fact: a WebGPU volume renderer that streams multiscale OME-Zarr over HTTP into a bounded GPU cache and renders in the browser with direct volume rendering (DVR), maximum intensity projection (MIP), isosurface and slice views. Untested idea: in-browser volume rendering for viewers.
3. **PCRD** (Sui et al.; [arXiv:2611.02204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02204), 1 Nov 2026; no code found). Sourced fact: SEM images at about 0.11 µm per pixel, available only in training, teach a micro-CT student at 2.63 µm by relational distillation. 89.97% of the reference fiber length is recovered, against 75.08% for the interpolated volume. Carbon-fiber composite. Untested idea: training-only high-resolution supervision for a lower-resolution CT model.
4. **CuPH** (Meng, Gu, Chambers, Chen; [arXiv:2611.01078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01078), 31 Oct 2026; no code link). Sourced fact: fast GPU persistent homology for 2D images, usable as a loss; 18.6 to 99.6 times faster than the CMP GPU baseline. CUDA only, so not on Apple MPS. Untested idea: a topology loss for 2D masks.
5. **Peripheral prototypical contrastive loss** (Shimaya, Shino, Saiko; [arXiv:2611.02305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02305), 1 Nov 2026; ISBI 2026; no code link). Sourced fact: a boundary-focused segmentation loss that is robust to noisy labels, on 2D medical images. Untested idea: a loss for noisy label edges.
6. **When Disagreement Labels Do Not Identify F1 Rankings** (Fernández-Barrios et al.; [arXiv:2611.01712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01712), 1 Nov 2026; code https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23647323). Sourced fact: after labeling only the patches where two models disagree, two agreement counts give exact bounds that certify an F1 ranking, or a choice within a set F1 tolerance, however the unlabeled patches turn out; the guarantee covers the evaluated batch only. Untested idea: label only disagreement patches when comparing two models on F1, and report when the bounds do not certify.
7. **OME-Zarr Open SciVis Datasets v2026.11.03** (Matthew McCormick; [Zenodo 23907588](https://zenodo.org/records/23907588), 3 Nov 2026, Apache-2.0). Sourced fact: public multiscale OME-Zarr test volumes, hosted on AWS S3 through the AWS Open Data Program. Untested idea: non-scroll test volumes for viewer and loader tests.

Also added, missing from this log before: **Apollo Restore** (McGovern et al.; [arXiv:2609.22455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22455), v1 18 Sep 2026, last version v4 7 Oct 2026; no code or model link). Sourced fact: a 24-billion-parameter LLM, fine-tuned from Mistral Small with a fill-in-the-middle objective, restores gaps in ancient Greek text without being told the gap length; 20 experts in a blind study judged its restorations at least as good as human ones in 77% of cases; it improves the published reading of PHerc. 1667. Untested idea: Greek gap filling as language context for a letter reader.
