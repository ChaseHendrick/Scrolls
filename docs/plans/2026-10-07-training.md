# Plan: train our own reader, three ways (decided 2026-10-07)

The user chose to pursue all three ideas below. Nothing has been trained yet. Each idea is measured the same way, written down here before any training: pixel AUC against the 20260918 human labels inside the supervision mask, with the reverse-depth map as the control (`kit auc`), on PHerc0841's three segments (a scroll in no candidate's training data), whole segment and the 640 px crops `scripts/mac-w045.sh` uses. A candidate counts as better only if it beats the best public reader on PHerc0841 in the forward direction without its reverse AUC rising toward its forward AUC.

## The three ideas

### 1. Train on PHerc1447, the scroll whose text was just found

- **Why:** the only scroll with text found at survey resolution (8.64 µm, 116 keV), the scan protocol of about half the First Letters scrolls. Public readers trained mostly on other protocols.
- **Data:** `YoussefMoNader/ink-8um-pherc1447-surfaces` (w058, w060, w062 layers and hand-refined labels, 2026-09-28).
- **Recipe:** v8in's own `finetune_loo_w062.py` already fine-tunes v8in on w058 + w060 (lr 1e-5 cosine, 10 epochs, effective batch 32, depth-reversed layers). Youssef released that model (`ink-8um-v8in-pherc1447-loo-w062`) but scored it only on PHerc1447. **First step costs no training: score his released fine-tune on PHerc0841.** Then, if useful, train on all three windings and score again.

### 2. A 1 µm teacher

- **Why:** d9v2 and Reader v2 learned from the team's 2.4 µm `new_canon` predictions. The team also publishes 1.1 µm "mrg" predictions on some segments (w045 has one), a sharper teacher. Their unmerged `distilled_3d_ink` branch points the same way.
- **Data:** inventory the bucket for segments with a 1.129 µm `ink-detection/*mrg*` map and a 9 µm surface volume; map the 1 µm canvas onto the 9 µm one (TAUIL's `align9.py` does the 2.4 µm case). Exclude PHerc0841 and the held-out set.
- **Recipe:** fine-tune from v8in or `ink_9um` (decided by v8in's PHerc0841 number) on the dense 1 µm targets, as d9v2 did with 2.4 µm targets.

### 3. Self-supervised pre-training on the target scans

- **Why:** the main failure is cross-scroll generalization. Pre-training on the eligible scans themselves, without labels, adapts the encoder to them.
- **Data:** `nestorvfx/vesuvius-ink256-pretrain-sheets` (flattened unlabelled sheets from 41 scans, including the eligible ones, made for masked-reconstruction pre-training; four evaluation segments held out).
- **Recipe:** masked reconstruction on v8in's encoder, then the fine-tune of idea 1 or 2. The most compute and the most risk.

### A possible fourth idea (not decided): self-training on a target

The PHerc. 1667 read used five rounds of scroll-specific pseudo-labelling ([arXiv:2606.29085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29085)). Doing the same on an eligible scroll could adapt a reader to it, but without labels on the target it can amplify the reader's own artifacts. It would first have to show, on PHerc0841 treated as if unlabelled, that it raises AUC against the held-back labels. Also note ([arXiv:2603.27698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27698)): fine surface relief carries ink signal only near 1 µm, so idea 2's 1 µm teacher may transfer less than hoped at 9 µm. See [`../logs/2026-10-07-literature.md`](../logs/2026-10-07-literature.md).

## Order and compute

1. Done before training: the PHerc0841 bar above; v8in's own number (user's Mac).
2. Idea 1, step one: score the released PHerc1447 fine-tune on PHerc0841 (Mac, about 30 min per segment, no training).
3. Build and smoke-test each data stage here on CPU (`finetune_loo_w062.py --smoke-test N`), so rented time is not spent on bugs.
4. Train ideas 1 and 2 on a rented GPU (d9v2 took 1.3 to 2 h on one RTX 3090; v8in is larger). Needs the user's decision on provider and budget; nothing is rented without it.
5. Idea 3 only after 1 and 2 show whether the domain matters.
